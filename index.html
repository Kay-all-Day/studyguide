Case Studies — MoFA Special Audit

Six case studies based on the Ghana Ministry of Food & Agriculture special audit (2025)

🏛️ Context — The MoFA Special Audit

In 2025, the Ghana Audit Service (with EY and PwC) conducted a special audit at MoFA triggered by a warehouse standoff in Tema. The audit uncovered missing grain, ghost companies, a firm paid in rice, and a complete breakdown of internal controls — exposing potential losses exceeding GH¢600 million in public funds.

Case Study 1 · Audit Evidence

The Vanishing Grains: What Is Evidence?

📋 Evidence Types · Sufficiency · Reliability
Scenario
  • MoFA contracted Danaasi Farms to supply over 34,000 tonnes of rice as part of a 2024 dry-spell relief programme.
  • The special audit team found that only 24,000 tonnes were said to have been distributed to eight regions — with 10,000 tonnes completely unaccounted for.
  • There were no Store Receipt Advices (SRAs) to confirm what was actually received, and no delivery notes to verify what was transported.
  • The chief procurement officer could not explain the fate of the missing 10,000 tonnes.
  • The Auditor-General's report stated there was "a complete breakdown of internal controls."
Questions
  1. Identify and explain the types of audit evidence the team would have sought to verify rice distribution. (6 marks)
  2. Evaluate the sufficiency and reliability of the evidence available in this scenario. (6 marks)
  3. The chief procurement officer could not explain the missing 10,000 tonnes. Explain what type of evidence this failure represents and its audit implications. (4 marks)
  4. Recommend specific evidence collection procedures the auditors should have employed. (4 marks)
Model Solution
Answer 1 — Types of Audit Evidence (6 marks)
  • Documentary Evidence: Store Receipt Advices (SRAs) to confirm receipt; Delivery Notes to verify quantities transported; Purchase orders and contracts; Payment records from Ministry of Finance.
  • Observed Physical Evidence: Physical inventory count of remaining rice stocks in warehouses; Direct inspection of distribution facilities across the eight regions.
  • Analytical Procedures (Results of Test Procedures): Cross-referencing purchase quantities against distribution records; Comparing tonnage contracted vs. distributed vs. missing.
  • Representations: Written/verbal statements from the chief procurement officer; Management statements about internal controls over grain distribution.
Answer 2 — Sufficiency and Reliability (6 marks)
  • Sufficiency: Evidence was grossly insufficient. Absence of SRAs and delivery notes meant there was no documentary basis to verify the 24,000 tonnes allegedly distributed. The standard is that another auditor reviewing the same evidence reaches the same conclusion — here, that is a complete accountability breakdown.
  • Reliability — Independence: RGD confirmation (external source confirming companies were unregistered) is highly reliable as it comes from an independent third party.
  • Reliability — Objectivity: The absence of SRAs is observable and objective — it requires no interpretation, making this a clear-cut evidentiary gap.
  • Reliability — Timing: Critical documents were not available at audit time. Electronic evidence may not be retrievable after passage of time — a serious concern flagged in the standards.
  • The SRA certified by the internal auditor without warehouse visits undermined reliability further — certification without factual basis is not valid evidence.
Answer 3 — Failure of Representations (4 marks)
  • This is a failure of representations — verbal/written statements management is expected to provide about operations and processes.
  • The inability to account for 10,000 tonnes means a key management representation cannot be obtained. Per standards, the auditor must inform audit management and governance bodies, and consider the impact on the opinion.
  • This elevates detection risk and may indicate deliberate concealment, requiring heightened professional skepticism and potential forensic referral.
Answer 4 — Recommended Procedures (4 marks)
  • Inspection: Demand all SRAs, delivery notes, and purchase orders from Danaasi Farms and logistics providers.
  • Observation: Physical counts of remaining grain stocks at all warehouses in all eight regions; observe loading/distribution operations.
  • CAATs/Analytical Procedures: Use GAS to cross-match payment records with delivery records; perform statistical sampling; use data analytics to identify patterns of discrepancy.
  • Inquiry and Third-Party Confirmation: Interview warehouse managers; obtain written confirmations from storage companies regarding actual receipt of grain.
  • Re-performance: Trace sample consignments from procurement order through to regional distribution point.
Case Study 2 · Audit Fieldwork

Locked Out: The Tema Warehouse Standoff

🔒 Access Restrictions · Independence · Fieldwork Procedures
Scenario
  • On September 2, 2025, Audit Service officials arrived at the Sikakrobea warehouse in Tema to verify rice stocks — and were denied entry. Management said they would only grant access to Danaasi Farms, not to MoFA or auditors.
  • MoFA officials returned on September 9 — without auditors — and retrieved 7,000 bags of rice for NADMO.
  • Sikakrobea reiterated they did not recognise MoFA's authority and would only deal with Danaasi Farms.
  • When auditors eventually gained access, they found a complete breakdown of internal controls, no SRAs, and no delivery notes.
Questions
  1. Explain how the denial of access represents an impairment to the audit function and how auditors should have responded. (6 marks)
  2. Analyse the significance of MoFA officials accessing the warehouse on September 9 without auditors present. Discuss the fieldwork implications. (6 marks)
  3. Describe the IT audit fieldwork steps the team should have followed from denial through to eventual access. (6 marks)
  4. Identify at least THREE red flags indicating irregularities or potential illegal acts. (2 marks)
Model Solution
Answer 1 — Impairment to Audit Function (6 marks)
  • Restrictions on access to records, personnel, equipment, or facilities are explicitly defined as impairments to auditor independence and objectivity. Denial of warehouse entry directly prevented the team from gathering physical (observation) and documentary evidence.
  • Required response: (1) Document the denial formally with dates, names, and reasons given; (2) Immediately escalate to audit management and the Audit Committee; (3) Inform Ministry of Finance (which issued the access directive); (4) Assess whether to postpone or proceed with alternative procedures; (5) Treat denial itself as potential evidence of concealment.
Answer 2 — Significance of September 9 Access Without Auditors (6 marks)
  • This is a textbook example of management overriding controls — one of the most critical fraud red flags. The removal of 7,000 bags before auditors could count inventory directly contaminated the physical evidence baseline.
  • Chain of Custody Broken: Physical assets were moved without auditor oversight, destroying the chain of custody essential for evidence integrity.
  • Fieldwork implication: All inventory counts after September 9 are compromised as baseline comparisons. Auditors must document this finding and seek alternative evidence (NADMO receipts, transport records for the 7,000 bags).
Answer 3 — Fieldwork Steps from Denial to Access (6 marks)
  • Step 1 — Document Denial: Record all details in workpapers — date, time, names, reasons. This documentation is itself evidence of obstruction.
  • Step 2 — Escalate: Inform audit management, Audit Committee, and Ministry of Finance to enforce the original access directive.
  • Step 3 — Alternative Evidence: While access is denied, pursue documentary evidence from Ministry of Finance payment records, contracts, and transport company records.
  • Step 4 — Entrance Meeting on Entry: Upon gaining access, conduct a formal entrance meeting to finalise scope, clarify authority, and identify points of contact.
  • Step 5 — Execute Fieldwork Plan: Acquire data → Test controls → Issue discovery and validation → Document results.
  • Step 6 — Risk Assessment Update: Elevate inherent and control risk ratings due to the access denial and unauthorised asset removal.
Answer 4 — Red Flags (2 marks)
  • Red Flag 1: Problems with receiving requested information or evidence — direct, deliberate denial of entry to auditors.
  • Red Flag 2: Management overrides of controls — MoFA officials removing assets without auditor presence.
  • Red Flag 3: Transactions not following normal approval cycles — rice distributed from a warehouse under audit dispute, without documented authorisation or auditor oversight.
  • Red Flag 4: Irregular management behaviour — Sikakrobea claiming only Danaasi Farms (not the contracting government ministry) had authority.
Case Study 3 · Audit Evidence

The Ghost Companies: Compliance & Third-Party Evidence

👻 Third-Party Confirmation · Documentary Evidence · CAATs
Scenario
  • Danaasi Farms was contracted for 34,000+ tonnes of rice; Rans Company Ltd was contracted to distribute grains nationwide.
  • Checks at the Registrar General's Department (RGD) confirmed neither company was registered under the Companies Act, 2019 (Act 992), nor as sole proprietorships or partnerships — both are ghost companies.
  • Rans Company Ltd was paid over GH¢50 million — the Auditor-General determined the fair cost should have been GH¢30.9 million, representing an overpayment of GH¢19.1 million.
Questions
  1. Explain why the RGD confirmation is considered highly reliable audit evidence, referencing reliability principles. (4 marks)
  2. Describe the documentary evidence needed to substantiate the ghost company finding. (6 marks)
  3. Explain how the GH¢19.1 million overpayment would be identified using analytical procedures and CAATs. (6 marks)
  4. What audit evidence supports a surcharge recommendation against responsible officers? (4 marks)
Model Solution
Answer 1 — Reliability of RGD Confirmation (4 marks)
  • Independence: RGD is an external source completely outside MoFA. Evidence from outside the enterprise is more reliable than internal evidence. The RGD has no interest in the audit outcome.
  • Objectivity: A company is either registered or it is not — there is no judgment or interpretation required, making this highly objective.
  • Qualifications: The RGD is the statutory authority for company registration under Act 992 — its officials are uniquely qualified and authoritative on this matter.
Answer 2 — Documentary Evidence for Ghost Companies (6 marks)
  • Copies of contracts signed between MoFA and both companies — examining signatories, registration numbers, and addresses.
  • Procurement records: RFPs, tender documents, bid evaluations, procurement committee minutes — to determine how unregistered companies passed procurement screening.
  • Payment records: payment vouchers, bank transfer confirmations, and MoF payment authorisations.
  • RGD formal written confirmation showing absence of registration for both entities under Act 992.
  • Identity documents provided by the companies during contracting (to verify fraudulent registration claims).
  • Electronic communications (emails, letters) revealing who controlled these entities.
Answer 3 — Analytical Procedures and CAATs for Overpayment (6 marks)
  • Benchmarking: The Auditor-General compared GH¢50M paid to the GH¢30.9M market benchmark for the same service — a standard benchmarking analytical procedure.
  • Recalculation: Re-performing the cost calculation: distance × tonnage × market transport rate = expected cost. Comparison to actual payment reveals the GH¢19.1M discrepancy.
  • CAATs — GAS: Extract all payment records to Rans Company Ltd; apply statistical analysis comparing payment amounts to contract terms; identify all payments exceeding approved contract values.
  • CAATs — Data Analytics: Cross-reference payment data with goods received data to identify payments where evidence of service delivery is absent.
  • CAATs — Duplicate Checking: GAS duplicate-checking to identify multiple payments for the same service period or reference.
Answer 4 — Evidence for Surcharge Recommendation (4 marks)
  • Payment authorization documents identifying the specific officers who approved payments to unregistered companies.
  • Procurement committee minutes and signing authorities identifying who approved contracts with ghost companies.
  • Written statements from responsible officers about their knowledge of the companies' registration status.
  • Quantified financial loss calculation (GH¢19.1M overpayment) with full workpaper support — dated, initialed, complete, and self-contained.
Case Study 4 · Audit Fieldwork

Controls Collapse: SoD and IAM in MoFA's Grain Programme

⚙️ Separation of Duties · IAM · Internal Controls Review
Scenario
  • A single chief procurement officer appeared to control the entire procurement cycle — from vendor selection through to payment authorisation — without independent review.
  • The procurement officer admitted there was an instruction to issue rice to Rans Company Ltd as payment, but could not produce any authorising memo.
  • MoFA's internal auditor certified a GH¢771.2 million maize delivery checklist for the Ministry of Finance without visiting the warehouse and without being able to identify its location.
  • There were no SRAs and no delivery notes to verify any transport.
Questions
  1. Identify and explain specific Separation of Duties (SoD) failures in the MoFA grain programme. (6 marks)
  2. Explain what an IT auditor would do when auditing SoD in this context. What specific tests would they perform? (6 marks)
  3. How does the internal auditor certifying documents without physical verification represent a failure of audit fieldwork standards? (4 marks)
  4. Recommend specific IT controls that could have prevented the identified failures. (4 marks)
Model Solution
Answer 1 — SoD Failures (6 marks)
  • Failure 1 — Unauthorised Asset Issuance: Rice was issued to Rans Company Ltd on verbal instruction without any written authorising memo. Assets must not be transferred without documented, approved authorisation — a core SoD principle.
  • Failure 2 — Custody and Authorisation Combined: The procurement officer had authority over both custody of assets (rice) and authorisation of transactions. Recording transactions, authorising transactions, and custody of assets must be separated.
  • Failure 3 — Recording Without Independent Verification: MoFA submitted SRAs for 100,000 tonnes of maize without physical verification. Receipt recording and physical custody should be independently verified activities.
  • Failure 4 — Internal Auditor's Oversight Role Abandoned: The internal auditor certifying a GH¢771.2M checklist without visiting the warehouse conflated operational and oversight roles.
Answer 2 — Auditing SoD: Procedures and Tests (6 marks)
  • Step 1 — Obtain and Understand Relationships: Obtain org charts, job descriptions, and system access rights to document responsibilities in the grain programme.
  • Step 2 — Analyze Job Functions: Identify which individuals could both authorise purchases and receive/custody goods — a direct SoD conflict.
  • Step 3 — Identify Compensating Controls: Assess whether management reviews, automated system controls, or regular reporting were designed to compensate for SoD weaknesses.
  • Step 4 — Test Access Rights: Use IAM audit procedures to verify system access rights reflected role separation; confirm payment authorisation required independent approval.
  • Step 5 — CAATs Tests: Using GAS, extract all transactions where a single individual both approved and recorded a transaction; verify asset issuances had documented, independent authorisations.
  • Step 6 — SoD Control Matrix: Compare roles against the SoD Control Matrix to identify which function combinations were improperly combined.
Answer 3 — Internal Auditor Certification Failure (4 marks)
  • Observation Failure: Observation is one of the four primary evidence collection techniques — it requires actually seeing and verifying what is being certified. Certifying GH¢771.2M of goods without visiting the warehouse violated this fundamental standard.
  • Evidence Validity: Evidence must be valid, factual, and supportable. Certification based on paperwork alone, when the auditor cannot identify the warehouse location, has no evidential value — it is worse than no evidence, as it creates a false impression of control.
  • Chain of Custody: There is no traceable path from goods received to the certification — the chain of custody principle in evidence management was completely broken.
Answer 4 — Recommended IT Controls (4 marks)
  • Automated Workflow Approvals: Implement a procurement management system requiring electronic, multi-level approval — no single individual can authorise and record a payment.
  • Three-Way Matching Control: Automate matching of purchase orders, goods received notes (SRAs), and payment vouchers — payment blocked unless all three documents match.
  • IAM System Controls: Restrict system access so the individual who authorises payment cannot also record receipt of goods — enforcing SoD in the system architecture.
  • Physical Inventory Management System: Require GPS-tagged, time-stamped photographic evidence of goods received uploaded to a government asset management system.
Case Study 5 · Audit Evidence

Paid in Rice: Evaluating Non-Standard Evidence

🌾 Corroborating Evidence · Analytical Procedures · Evidence Sufficiency
Scenario
  • Rans Company Ltd — a transport and logistics firm — was not paid in Ghana Cedis. Instead, it was given 7,311 tonnes of rice (valued at GH¢11.6 million at market price) to settle a debt of GH¢6.28 million for haulage services.
  • This effectively granted the company a 41% discount at taxpayer expense.
  • The chief procurement officer admitted there was an instruction to issue the rice, but could not produce any authorising memo.
  • The Auditor-General is now demanding officers involved be surcharged with the market price of the rice.
Questions
  1. Explain how corroborating evidence would have been used to support this finding. (6 marks)
  2. Discuss the evidence collection procedures used to identify and quantify the overvaluation. (6 marks)
  3. The absence of an authorising memo is a critical evidentiary gap. Explain its significance and how auditors should address it. (4 marks)
  4. What makes this finding adequate for a surcharge recommendation? Discuss evidence sufficiency. (4 marks)
Model Solution
Answer 1 — Corroborating Evidence (6 marks)
  • Source 1 — Management Representation: The procurement officer's verbal admission that rice was issued to Rans Company was corroborated by physical and documentary evidence from other sources.
  • Source 2 — Physical Evidence: Physical counts of rice removed from the warehouse (7,311 tonnes) confirmed the scale of the transfer.
  • Source 3 — Documentary Evidence: Warehouse issue records and transport records for the 7,311 tonnes corroborated the physical transfer.
  • Source 4 — External Market Price: Independent market price survey for rice at the time of issuance corroborated the GH¢11.6M valuation — this is external, independent evidence of value.
  • Source 5 — Haulage Contract/Invoice: The original haulage debt of GH¢6.28M was corroborated by the contract and invoices from Rans Company Ltd.
  • The convergence of management representations, physical observation, documentary evidence, and external market data from different, independent origins makes the finding robustly supported.
Answer 2 — Evidence Collection for Overvaluation (6 marks)
  • Recalculation: Multiply rice volume (7,311 tonnes) × market price per tonne = GH¢11.6M. Compare to outstanding debt of GH¢6.28M to calculate the GH¢5.32M excess (41% over-settlement).
  • Analytical Procedures — Ratio Analysis: Comparing rice market value to debt value directly produces the 41% ratio revealing the overvaluation.
  • Inspection of Financial Records: Review of haulage contracts, invoices, and payment records to establish the authorised debt amount from a legitimate source.
  • External Market Price Confirmation: Obtain rice market prices from an independent source (e.g., Ghana Grains Council) at the date of issuance to objectively value the 7,311 tonnes.
  • CAATs — Data Matching: Use GAS to match warehouse issue records against payment/debt settlement records to identify all non-cash settlements.
Answer 3 — Missing Authorising Memo Significance (4 marks)
  • The authorising memo would be the primary documentary control identifying who had authority to approve non-cash settlement, that the decision was made at the appropriate approval level, and that public procurement regulations were followed.
  • Its absence is a failure of the authorisation control in the SoD framework — assets transferred without documented authority.
  • Auditor Response: (1) Document absence as a control deficiency finding; (2) Issue formal inquiry to the officer and their supervisor; (3) Expand search to electronic communications (emails, messages) that may corroborate the instruction; (4) Treat the absence as elevating fraud risk and report as a significant deficiency.
Answer 4 — Evidence Sufficiency for Surcharge (4 marks)
  • Sufficient evidence is adequate, convincing, and leads another auditor to form the same conclusion. For a surcharge: a quantifiable financial loss, a responsible party, and absence of authorisation must be demonstrated.
  • The evidence is sufficient because: physical transfer of 7,311 tonnes is documented; market value (GH¢11.6M) is objectively verified; debt amount (GH¢6.28M) is contractually established; the GH¢5.32M loss is mathematically calculable; and the procurement officer admitted awareness of the instruction.
  • All evidence must be in workpapers that are dated, initialed, relevant, clear, complete, and self-contained to support the formal surcharge recommendation.
Case Study 6 · Combined Application

Maize Mismanagement: When the Auditor Cannot Verify

🌽 Fieldwork + Evidence · GH¢653M Overstatement · Internal Audit Failure
Scenario
  • MoFA submitted SRAs as evidence of delivery of 100,000 tonnes of maize worth GH¢771.2 million to the Ministry of Finance for payment.
  • However, only 11,900 tonnes were actually supplied and distributed — a discrepancy of over 88,000 tonnes representing a potential GH¢653 million overstatement.
  • The SRA was supported by a checklist certified by MoFA's internal auditor, who admitted she had not visited the maize warehouse since February of the previous year and could not identify its location.
  • The Auditor-General's team was consequently unable to verify the existence of any maize stocks.
Questions
  1. Explain the combined audit fieldwork and evidence failures that allowed a GH¢653 million overstatement to reach the payment stage. (8 marks)
  2. Describe the specific fieldwork procedures the Auditor-General's team should have used to verify the existence of maize stocks. (6 marks)
  3. What lessons does this case provide about the reliability of internal audit certifications as audit evidence? How should external auditors treat such certifications? (6 marks)
Model Solution
Answer 1 — Combined Fieldwork and Evidence Failures (8 marks)
  • Fieldwork Failure 1 — No Physical Observation: The most basic fieldwork procedure — physically observing goods claimed received — was not performed before the SRA was submitted. Observation of physical items is a primary evidence collection technique; its omission is catastrophic.
  • Fieldwork Failure 2 — Internal Audit Independence Compromised: The internal auditor certified without performing the verification she was certifying, violating the principle that auditors must have direct, independent evidence. The internal audit function became a facilitator of misrepresentation.
  • Evidence Failure 1 — Invalid Documentary Evidence: The SRA was based on a checklist with no physical basis. Documentary evidence is only reliable if underlying facts are independently verified — here, the SRA was certified without evidence.
  • Evidence Failure 2 — Three-Way Match Absent: A properly designed control would require matching purchase order, delivery note, and SRA. Any element requiring independent physical verification would have surfaced the discrepancy before payment.
  • Evidence Failure 3 — Timing: The internal auditor had not visited since February — her knowledge was at least 7+ months old. Evidence reliability requires it reflects the period under examination.
  • Systemic Control Failure: No compensating controls (no independent inventory system, no random verification, no MoF requirement for independent certification) allowed false documentation to pass unchallenged to a GH¢771.2M payment request.
Answer 2 — Fieldwork Procedures for Verifying Maize Stocks (6 marks)
  • Observation — Physical Inventory Count: Conduct a direct, independent physical count of maize stocks across all claimed warehouses — the primary fieldwork procedure for verifying existence of physical assets.
  • Third-Party Confirmation: Obtain written confirmation from all warehouse operators regarding quantities of maize held on MoFA's behalf. Third-party confirmation is more reliable than MoFA's own records.
  • Inspection and Document Tracing: Trace each SRA back to supporting delivery notes, transport manifests, and purchase records — each tonne of claimed receipt must have a traceable documentary chain.
  • Analytical Procedures: Compare claimed 100,000 tonnes against available storage capacity, previous period records, and distribution records for the eight regions.
  • Re-performance: Re-perform the stock count at each claimed storage location; compare findings against the SRA figures submitted to MoF.
  • CAATs — Data Analytics: Extract and analyse all receipts and distribution records using GAS; cross-match supplier delivery data with storage records to identify the overstatement point.
Answer 3 — Reliability of Internal Audit Certifications (6 marks)
  • Lesson 1: Internal evidence is inherently less reliable. A core reliability principle is that external evidence is more reliable than internal. An internal auditor's certification, while authoritative internally, is subject to institutional pressures and conflicts of interest.
  • Lesson 2: Independence and qualifications matter. The MoFA internal auditor could not identify the warehouse location — calling into question both her independence and qualifications for certifying this specific evidence.
  • Lesson 3: Verification must be direct. Certification is only valid when based on direct, independent observation. A certificate without a factual basis creates a false impression of control — worse than no evidence.
  • How External Auditors Should Treat Such Certifications: (1) Treat as corroborating, not conclusive, evidence — always seek independent physical verification; (2) Evaluate scope and independence of the internal audit function before relying on its work; (3) For significant assertions (GH¢771.2M), require independent physical verification regardless of internal certifications; (4) If internal audit evidence appears unreliable, escalate the risk assessment and expand substantive testing; (5) Document the basis for any reliance placed on internal audit work in audit workpapers.

📚 Top 10 Exam Themes to Master
  • Four types of audit evidence: Documentary, Observed, Analytical Procedures, Representations
  • Evidence reliability factors: Independence > Objectivity > Qualifications > Timing
  • Fieldwork sequence: Acquire Data → Test Controls → Issue Discovery → Document Results
  • SoD: Three conflicting functions = Recording, Authorisation, Custody of Assets
  • CAATs: GAS functions, Test Data (ITF/BCSE), Data Analytics applications
  • Workpaper standards: Dated, Initialed, Relevant, Clear, Complete, Self-contained, Retrievable
  • Fraud red flags: management overrides, consistent overperformance, transaction bypasses
  • Audit risk components: Inherent Risk × Control Risk × Detection Risk
  • Audit opinion types: Unqualified, Qualified, Adverse, Disclaimed
  • ISSAI 5300 = overarching IT audit standard; ISO 38500 = IT governance; ISO 27000 = security
✅ CIA Triad vs. Financial Assertions
  • CIA (IT controls): Confidentiality · Integrity · Availability
  • Financial Assertions: Occurrence · Completeness · Accuracy · Cut-off · Classification · Existence · Valuation
  • The linkage: IT control objectives (CIA) can impact financial assertions — IT audit findings must be mapped to financial audit implications where possible.
